# Generalization presentation script (about 4–5 minutes)

## Slide 1 — Question and design (about 1:20)

My generalization question is whether fine-tuning changes robustness to irrelevant preceding context. We compare the published Qwen3-0.6B base model with our seed-17, epoch-2 LoRA checkpoint on short-answer SQuAD 1.1 questions. The answer-bearing passage stays intact; we add about one thousand unrelated tokens before it and compare exact match with the original input. Both models see the same question, prompt, and distractor. Generation is greedy with a 64-token cap, and there are two fixed distractor seeds. We started with 90 questions, two from each of 45 articles. After inspecting those results, we planned a follow-up on the remaining 810 questions from those same articles. Before the first run, we predicted that LoRA's exact match would fall by at least eight percentage points at this length.

## Slide 2 — Initial 90-question result (about 1:20)

This first figure shows exact match on the left and token F1 on the right as we add approximately 512 and 1,024 tokens. The circles average two fixed distractor seeds; the x marks show each seed. At 1,024 tokens, LoRA exact match changed from 78.89 to 76.67 percent, a drop of 2.22 points. That did not meet our original prediction of an eight-point drop. The article-bootstrap interval for LoRA's change runs from negative 8.89 to positive 3.89 points, so the small point estimate is still uncertain. The base model fell more in the point estimates, from 20.00 to 12.22 percent. But the direct difference between the two models' declines was positive 5.56 points with an interval from negative 3.89 to positive 15.56. Because that interval crosses zero, this initial sample did not establish that LoRA was more robust. We therefore tested the remaining fixed questions, without changing the checkpoint or distractor seeds.

## Slide 3 — 810-question follow-up and conclusion (about 1:40)

The second figure shows the new 810-question validation. The base model's exact match fell from 22.96 to 14.07 percent, a drop of 8.89 points. LoRA fell from 76.79 to 71.30 percent, a drop of 5.49 points. Our primary paired measure is the LoRA change minus the base-model change, with both models compared on the same question and the two seeds averaged. It is positive 3.40 points. Its 95 percent article-cluster bootstrap interval is 0.93 to 5.80 points, entirely above zero. The right panel contrasts that with the wide, inconclusive interval from the initial 90 questions. So we can say that this checkpoint lost less exact match in this particular SQuAD preceding-context setting. We also reviewed mistakes: among 149 LoRA records that changed from correct to incorrect, 138 wrong answer strings appeared only in the original passage. Only two appeared solely in the distractor; two appeared in both passages and seven matched neither exactly. For example, an ABC answer switched from “The Dating Game” to “The Newlywed Game,” both in the original passage. The new questions were still drawn from the same 45 articles, with two fixed distractors and one LoRA training seed. We cannot claim cross-domain transfer or robustness on unseen articles.
